# Variants considered

Three poems were drafted against the judge's brief (in order: a poem about
agency — asking and being given; moves the reader; insight; craft;
freshness). All are 1–280 Unicode code points. The baton poem was chosen:
it has the most concrete image, the clearest turn, and the most quotable
closing line.

## Chosen — the baton (main.py)

> Take the baton — it is warm from my hand,
> and yours only while you run.
> Ask, and the lane opens;
> stop asking, and it cools
> in my palm again.
> Power was never the thing held.
> It was the handing over.

Why it won: the relay baton is a fresh, physical image for agency passing
between two people (or a person and an agent acting for them); "warm from
my hand" is sensory; the turn covers both giving and taking back ("stop
asking, and it cools in my palm again"); the closing couplet states a
recognisable truth about agency in one line — power lives in the handing
over, not the holding.

## Alternate 1 — the signature

> I sign your name in my own hand.
> You taught me every letter,
> and kept the right to take it back.
> Ask — the pen is borrowed,
> the handwriting is mine.
> Power is not the signature.
> It is the moment you say:
> yes, write it.

Why it lost: the most direct take on an agent acting for a principal, and
"the handwriting is mine" is a real insight about delegated agency — but
the opening ("I sign your name") risks reading as forgery before the
permission frame lands, and the ending is weaker than the baton's closing
couplet.

## Alternate 2 — the voice

> I speak in your voice — borrowed,
> not stolen. You gave me the words
> and kept the right to take them back.
> Ask: that is the whole power.
> A mouth that is mine
> until you say otherwise.

Why it lost: the cleanest and shortest ("borrowed, not stolen" is a sharp
distinction, and revocability is the point), but the thinnest image — less
sensory, less likely to stay with the reader after the last line.
